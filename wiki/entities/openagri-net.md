# OpenAgriNet (OAN)

**Type:** System
**Role:** Digital public goods architecture / Open network infrastructure
**Geography:** India (anchor); Ethiopia (first international deployment); designed for global replication
**Contact or reference:** Not publicly documented in source material

## What they are

OpenAgriNet (OAN) is the open-source digital public goods architecture that underlies the MahaVistaar, Bharat-VISTAAR, Amul Sarlaben, and Ethiopia ATI agricultural AI deployments. OAN consists of two structural elements: open-source building blocks (the open network protocols, agentic AI architecture, voice-first access layer, consent frameworks, and data integration standards) and a collaboration blueprint (the governance model, enabler network, institutional arrangements, and documented organisational learning). Neither element works without the other: building blocks without the collaboration blueprint produce technical deployments without institutional trust; the blueprint without the building blocks produces coordination without delivery. OAN does not build what already exists in a country or state context — it connects it through a common network layer.

## Deployments they are associated with

- [MahaVistaar](../pathways/mahavistaar.md) — Anchor deployment; produced the OAN DPG layer as it exists today
- [Bharat-VISTAAR](../pathways/bharat-vistaar.md) — National OAN integration layer for India
- [Amul Sarlaben](../pathways/amul-sarlaben.md) — OAN DPG layer adapted to cooperative context
- [Bihar Krishi](../pathways/bihar-krishi.md) — Connected to OAN through Bharat-VISTAAR
- [Ethiopia ATI](../pathways/ethiopia-ati.md) — OAN architecture adapted internationally

## When this entity is relevant to an adopter

An adopter should investigate OAN when: (1) planning an agricultural AI advisory deployment and asking whether to build from scratch or reuse existing architecture; (2) designing a federated data approach for agricultural institutional data across multiple government agencies; (3) seeking open-source governance frameworks, deployment playbooks, or failure mode libraries from prior deployments; or (4) evaluating the 3-week (Amul) to 3-month (Ethiopia) to 9-month (MahaVistaar) deployment timeline range and wanting to understand what each reflects about starting conditions.
