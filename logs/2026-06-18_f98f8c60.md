### Assistant

Yes — and it's worth clarifying because it's one of the least obvious findings in this knowledge, and it turned out to matter more than the technology in every deployment.

**The core idea:** when a farmer gets an answer from the system, *who does that answer appear to come from?* The consistent finding is that the AI should never present itself as "an AI" or as a standalone product. It speaks **as an institution the farmer already trusts** — and every recommendation carries that institution's identity behind it.

The reason this matters comes from a distinction farmers make that the deployments documented clearly: **there's a difference between information and *trusted* information.** The same advice — say, which pesticide to use — sounds like a sales pitch coming from a commercial seller, but sits as reliable when it comes from the state agriculture university or the department's extension service. The words can be identical. What changes whether the farmer *acts* on it is whose voice it's in.

So the AI is positioned as a **delivery layer, not an authority.** The institution stands behind every answer; the AI just makes that institution's knowledge reachable by phone. It doesn't replace the institution's accountability — it carries it.

A few concrete examples of how this showed up:

- **MahaVistaar** speaks as the Maharashtra Department of Agriculture, and cites the state agricultural universities for specific crop advice. That combination answers two different questions a farmer has at once: *"who's responsible if this is wrong?"* (the department) and *"does this come from someone who actually knows farming?"* (the university).
- **Amul Sarlaben** speaks as Amul — and goes further, with a deliberately chosen Gujarati woman's name, signalling it's built *for* the woman dairy farmer, in her language. The trust there is unusually deep because every farmer who calls has had an Amul milk collector at her door that same morning.
- **Bihar Krishi** speaks as the state agriculture department, and leans on national award recognition as an extra signal — *"independent evaluators verified this works,"* not just *"the government says so."*

There's a second, subtler layer worth flagging for your design: **voice design is itself trust design.** The choice of *language*, *channel* (a phone call versus an app needing a smartphone and data), and even a *name* all signal to the farmer whether this was built for them. An AI that speaks their dialect on a basic phone, reachable by dialling a number that feels like calling a person, carries trust that a smartphone app simply cannot.

**Why this is a design decision for your initiative, not a detail:**

There's a hard precondition hiding in here. For the AI to speak in a trusted institution's voice, **that institution has to be willing to say "this runs in my name" and be accountable for what the system tells farmers.** That's not automatic — it means the institution is putting its credibility on the line for outputs generated by AI. So this connects directly back to your earlier question about which institution runs it: the trust question and the accountability question are the *same* question.

And there's a real failure mode your funding criteria can guard against: a state could build something technically excellent that speaks as "[Product Name] AI" — a fresh brand with no existing credibility — and find farmers simply don't act on its advice. The deployments suggest that where the trusted institutional relationship is weak or absent, uptake stalls *regardless of technical quality*.

So for your design, a useful gating question becomes: **"Which institution will the AI speak as, does that institution already have credibility with the target farmers, and is it willing to be accountable for the outputs?"** A state that can't answer all three has an adoption problem coming, no matter how good the build is.

One honest caveat: every documented case used a *government or cooperative* institution as the trusted voice. Whether a private or market actor can serve as the trusted voice in a public-interest farming context isn't something this knowledge covers — so if any of your states are considering a private-sector-led model, that's genuinely untested territory I can't speak to.
