## The most relevant pathway for you: Ethiopia ATI

Ethiopia ATI is your closest reference — the first OAN deployment outside India, three months from commitment to launch. What makes it your best starting point:

- **The adaptation question, not the build question.** Ethiopia's evaluators asked "what does adapting this require here?" rather than "can this be built?" The architecture, governance frameworks, language pipeline methodology, data connector approach, and failure mode library all transferred. What needed local work: language pipeline (Oromo and others, not Marathi), institutional context, and the data connector (Fayda national ID rather than India's AgriStack). You will have to assemble your Senegalese ecosystem from scratch — the four-layer structure (institutional/governance, technology/AI, structured data, knowledge/documents) holds constant, but every specific partner changes. Budget for this; it's the longest non-technology task.

## The two decisions to make before anything technical

From the dimension-shifts evidence, two early decisions unlock the rest:

**1. Who is your network operator?** (Dimension D) — which institution can say "this runs in my name" and align the others? In Ethiopia this was ATI, a *national transformation institute* whose mandate spans government functions. That cross-institutional authority is exactly what made three months possible — because no single ministry could align the Ministry of Agriculture, meteorological services, and research institutes alone. **Ask yourself: does Senegal have an equivalent cross-ministry body, or will your Ministry of Agriculture have to align weather services and research institutes itself?** If the latter, that institutional alignment will likely be your longest task, not the technology.

**2. Whose voice does the AI speak in?** (Trust Architecture) — your farmers will trust the AI only as much as they trust the institution it speaks for. Identify which institution already has credibility with Senegalese farmers — an extension service, a cooperative, a ministry — and confirm it will bear accountability for outputs. Solve this before the technology question.

## Access design (your hardest-to-reach user)

Voice-first in local languages on any phone — including basic feature phones and landlines — is the consistent pattern across MahaVistaar (Marathi), Amul (Gujarati), and Ethiopia (Oromo). Inclusion is a design constraint from the start, not a feature added later. For Senegal this means designing for the Wolof-speaking farmer on a feature phone first.
