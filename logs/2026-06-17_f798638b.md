Good — that's the MahaVistaar pattern, and it's the most fully documented pathway in the wiki. As a neutral organization, your role maps closely to what EkStep Foundation played: technical/orchestration partner enabling the state to deploy under its own authority.

Three things to anchor your design:

**1. Don't build from scratch — start from the OAN DPG library.**
MahaVistaar took 9 months as the pioneer. Ethiopia (a state-equivalent national deployment) took 3 months by adapting, not rebuilding. The architecture, governance frameworks, language pipeline methodology, and failure mode library all transfer. Your first real task is a **DPG audit**: what applies to your state's context unmodified, and what needs adaptation. Start by contacting EkStep / OpenAgriNet ([DPG Reuse Patterns](synthesis/dpg-reuse-patterns.md)).

**2. Your hardest work is the 70%, not the technology.**
Across all deployments, ~70% of the challenge is institutional, ecosystem, workforce, and operating model — not the AI ([70-30 Split](synthesis/70-30-split.md)). As a neutral org, the two things you must help the state nail early:
- **Network operator** — which state institution says "this runs in my name" and authorizes data-sharing across its own systems. In MahaVistaar this was the Department of Agriculture, with a named Agri Secretary sponsor and nodal officers *before* build began.
- **Framing** — transformation initiative, not technology project. A project framing gets pilot funding and dies. (Bihar embedded theirs in a multi-year Agriculture Roadmap.)

**3. Workforce training is a deployment dependency, not an afterthought.**
Bihar Krishi trained 15,000+ extension workers across 38 districts as part of the rollout. The failure mode is training too late ([Failure Modes](synthesis/failure-modes.md)).

One question to sharpen this for you: **what's the state's starting data situation — do they have existing fragmented sources to connect (like Maharashtra), or are they closer to building from a low base (like Bihar)?** That determines your timeline and whether you follow the MahaVistaar or Bihar Krishi route.

This is very conceptual. When I need to design the initiative, tell me concretely what I should do

---

### Assistant

Fair. Here's a concrete sequence drawn from how MahaVistaar and the OAN deployments actually ran. I'll keep it to the order of moves, not theory.

**Step 1 — Secure the network operator and sponsor (before any build)**
- Get a named senior sponsor in the state — MahaVistaar had an Agri Secretary. Not a committee, a person.
- Get them to nominate nodal officers across agriculture, IT, and field operations.
- Get written authorization for data-sharing across the department's own systems and its agricultural universities. This is the single decision that unblocks everything else.
*(Source: MahaVistaar pathway, Dimension C; OAN Reusable Assets, Institutional ownership model.)*

**Step 2 — Start data-sharing negotiations immediately, in parallel**
- Treat inter-institutional data agreements as the critical path, not a side task. Begin before technical build.
- Use the OAN data-sharing agreement framework template rather than drafting from zero.
*(Source: OAN Reusable Assets, Solution Patterns — Institution.)*

**Step 3 — Run the DPG audit**
- Sit with EkStep / OpenAgriNet. For each of the seven DPGs, decide: reuse as-is, or adapt. The language pipeline and the ecosystem will always need local adaptation; the architecture and governance frameworks usually transfer.
*(Source: DPG Reuse Patterns.)*

**Step 4 — Frame the problem from the farmer inward, bounded**
- Pick one or two crops, a few districts, one or two languages. MahaVistaar's "bounded ambition" — not a statewide cold launch.
- Write the problem statement as a specific farmer's question the current system can't answer. If the state can't articulate this, stop and fix it first.

**Step 5 — Map the four-layer ecosystem**
- Institutional/governance, technology/AI, structured data, knowledge/documents. MahaVistaar had 54 enablers; you'll have your own set. Use the 54-enabler map as the template, not the answer.
*(Source: MahaVistaar Reusable Toolkit.)*

**Step 6 — Build workforce training into the rollout plan, not after**
- Plan extension-worker training district by district, scheduled before user-facing launch. Bihar's 15,000-worker programme is the reference model.

**Step 7 — Decide the operating model before the pilot**
- Who owns steady-state ops after pilot? What's the funding line after pilot money ends? Reference cost: ~$250k to set up, ~$250k/year to run.
*(Source: 70-30 Split; MahaVistaar Dimension F.)*

**Step 8 — Launch bounded, sequence plus-one**
- Advisory first. Add capabilities one at a time. Never plus-ten.
