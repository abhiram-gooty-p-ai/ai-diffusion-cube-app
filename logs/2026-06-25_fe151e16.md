Good — that's a concrete thing to work on. There's a real deployment that did exactly this: Project Astitva in Nandurbar, Maharashtra, which enabled Dehvali Bhili (a tribal language with over 9 lakh speakers, no prior digital dataset) for voice AI and integrated it into the state agricultural advisory platform. Most of what a playbook would need is grounded in that experience plus the broader voice AI deployment lessons.

If I map the actual structure that emerged from that work, a playbook would likely move through these stages:

**Before any data collection** — the prerequisite framing that language enablement is its own deployment activity, not a downstream technical task. This is where you decide which language, confirm the downstream use case, and critically, decide inbound vs outbound scope upfront (Astitva and MahaVISTAAR both learned that retrofitting outbound into an inbound-only architecture is expensive). You also map the full actor ecosystem here — a recurring lesson is that a neutral orchestrator must convene everyone, because key technical partners (IIT Madras in Astitva's case) may not even know the effort is happening.

**Building the dataset from zero** — the community-participatory model: recruiting native speakers, a three-tier quality structure (contributors → checkers → validators), and fair, transparent compensation. Two hard-won data lessons belong here: collect conversational speech separately from read-aloud speech, and collect telephony-grade 8kHz audio, not just clean 16kHz — because the model degrades on real phone lines otherwise.
