Pre-select the two or three most generative questions per dimension as the discussion focus, and let the rest sit as reference. That keeps your structure intact and just manages the depth-versus-coverage tradeoff.

So with two groups, you've got two clean options:

**Option A — two groups, three dimensions each, focused questions.** Your original plan, but you curate which questions each group actually discusses so they go deep rather than rushing. Coverage of all six dimensions across the room is preserved.

**Option B — two groups, two dimensions each.** Drops to four dimensions covered total. You'd lose two dimensions from the room's collective work — which is only worth it if there are two you're comfortable handling in the presentation or synthesis rather than the working session.

I'd lean Option A for your goal, because you want participants to leave with an approach across the *whole* journey, and dropping two dimensions entirely would leave gaps in that arc.

The three-groups idea only becomes attractive if you have enough participants that two groups would be too large for everyone to actually talk — beyond roughly 7–8 per group, the quieter people stop contributing. How many participants are you expecting? That's the real input for the group-count decision, and it's separate from the dimensions question.
