### Assistant

This is a rich situation — enabling a low-resource tribal language for a voice assistant is a well-documented challenge, and there's directly relevant lived experience to draw on. Before I suggest a workshop design, let me make sure I understand the core of it.

The closest documented experience here is Project Astitva in Nandurbar, Maharashtra — a district effort to bring Dehvali Bhili (a tribal language with no prior digital corpus) into a voice AI system and integrate it with MahaVISTAAR. That's almost exactly your team's situation: a tribal language, no existing models, an existing voice assistant to extend. So your workshop can be grounded in real patterns rather than first principles.

The single most important framing to land in the workshop is this: **enabling Soliga is not primarily a technical task — it's a data creation task that has to start before any application work.** Soliga almost certainly has native speakers but no written corpus, no recordings, no transcriptions. The Astitva experience treated language data creation as a *prerequisite deployment activity*, not a downstream technical step. If your team walks in thinking "how do we plug Soliga into the assistant," and walks out thinking "how do we build the Soliga dataset, with the community, before we build anything," that's a successful workshop.

A few things I'd want to understand to tailor the design:

- Does the team see this as a technical extension, or do they already recognise the community data-creation dimension? That tells you how much reframing the workshop needs to do up front.
- Who currently has a relationship with the Soliga community — the ASHA workers themselves, the tribal welfare department, an NGO? Trust-building with the community is documented as something that has to happen *before* data collection, not alongside it.

Where would you like to start — shall I sketch a session-by-session flow, or first work through the key decisions the team needs to reach by the end?
