The wiki doesn't hold a general model-selection methodology — and I'd be wary of giving you one that isn't grounded in a real deployment. But it does hold something more useful for your situation: the lived architecture experience of MahaVistaar, which serves comparable scale and documented its model choices in detail. Here's what that evidence says.

**The wiki's core finding reframes your question.** Across every documented deployment, model choice is treated as *less* important than architecture choice (dimension shift B1: "model as a replaceable component"). The design principle from MahaVistaar: no AI model is permanent; the infrastructure beneath — institutional data sources, open network protocols, farmer registries — stays stable as models evolve. So the first thing to get right isn't *which model* but *whether you can swap the model without rebuilding*. If model selection is a high-stakes, hard-to-reverse decision in your architecture, that's the problem to fix first.

**What MahaVistaar actually ran (your closest scale reference):**
- Primary: a fine-tuned **Qwen3.5-27B** (open-source), self-hosted on 4×H100 GPUs via vLLM
- Independent moderation: **GPT-OSS Safeguard 20B** on 2×H100s, fully decoupled from the advisory model
- Fallback: **Azure OpenAI**, retained for reliability, not as primary inference

**The cost evidence is the sharpest input to your decision.** MahaVistaar ran entirely on GPT-4.1 via Azure for several months at a volume-weighted **₹9.4 per question** (Nov 2025 production data, 216,000 queries). The self-hosted fine-tuned path achieves **~₹0.05 per question at full utilisation — a ~180× reduction.** At your 25k queries/day, that difference compounds fast. The migration was driven by an Azure trajectory headed toward ~₹6 lakh/day at voice scale.

**Two MahaVistaar specifics that matter for your scale planning:**
- *The cost lever is input tokens, not output.* Input token cost was 79.7% of total — so prefix caching, not output efficiency, is the primary cost optimisation. Advisory queries dominated cost (67% of spend on 57% of volume) because they chain multiple tool calls.
- *Peak load policy: "latency over queue depth."* The vLLM primary is capped at 100 concurrent calls per endpoint; saturated requests route instantly to Azure fallback rather than queuing. Load tests at 10/25/60 users/minute produced no fallback events.

**What I can't tell you from the wiki:** whether Qwen3.5-27B fits *your* domain — that depends on your use case, language, and accuracy requirements, none of which you've described. MahaVistaar's choice was for Marathi agricultural advisory with university-attributed content. If your domain differs, the model evaluation benchmarks and adversarial test sets (500 attack patterns) MahaVistaar produced are reusable, but the fit decision is yours.

To point you at the right evidence, it'd help to know: **what are your users doing** (advisory? discovery? transactions?), **what language(s)**, and **how reversible is your current architecture if you pick wrong?** That last one is, per the wiki, the real decision.
