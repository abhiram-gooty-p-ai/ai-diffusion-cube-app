Before I give you a checklist, let me understand your situation a little better — because the right questions depend on who you're serving and where you are in the journey.

When you say "investment related processes of govt" — do you mean helping citizens/businesses navigate government investment schemes and approvals (like how to apply, what they're eligible for, what the process is)? And who is the user — individual citizens, small businesses, larger investors? That shapes almost everything else.

That said, since you asked for a checklist, here's a starting frame drawn from patterns that recur across documented public-interest AI advisory deployments. These are the questions worth sitting with:

**On the problem itself**
- Can you name the specific person whose process gets easier, and exactly what's broken for them today in the current (non-AI) system?
- Are your success metrics about usage (queries answered) or about outcomes (applications actually completed, approvals obtained)?

**On trust and authority — this is the big one for government advisory**
- Which institution does your bot speak *on behalf of*, and does that institution have credibility with your users? In every documented deployment, the AI borrows trust from a trusted institution — it never earns it on its own.
- Who has the authority to decide what the bot can and cannot claim about eligibility, process, or rules? When it states an eligibility criterion or a procedural requirement, is that traceable to a named, authoritative source — or is it answering from model memory? For investment processes, a wrong eligibility or compliance answer is high-stakes.

**On data and architecture**
- Is the underlying process/scheme information owned by other departments? What does it take to get authorised access — and have you started those conversations early (they're usually the critical path)?
- How will you keep the bot current when rules, thresholds, or eligibility criteria change? Stale answers in a regulatory domain are dangerous.

**On the institution behind it**
- Is this framed as a one-time project or a capability the institution owns long-term? Who owns it when the person driving it moves on?

I'll stop there rather than overwhelm you — these are the highest-leverage ones. The trust/authority cluster is where government advisory deployments most often get into trouble, because a confident wrong answer about a compliance requirement damages the very institution whose name the bot carries.
