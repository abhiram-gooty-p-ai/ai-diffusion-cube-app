### Assistant

This is much clearer now — and it's a meaningfully different design problem than a citizen-facing bot. Your Udyami Mitras are a frontline workforce: professionally qualified intermediaries who carry your advisory into their daily practice with investors. That changes where the risks sit.

Let me reflect back what I'm hearing: the core problem isn't that the policy information doesn't exist — it's fragmented across departments and not easily reachable by the Udyami Mitra at the moment they're advising an investor. That's a *data connection* problem, not a *data collection* problem. This is exactly the framing MahaVistaar started from in Maharashtra — a farmer's question needed weather, market price, and crop advisory from institutions that had never been designed to talk to each other. The intervention was wiring them together, not building a new database.

A few things worth thinking through, given your specific user:

**On the workforce dimension — this is your distinctive challenge.** Your Udyami Mitras are qualified professionals whose authority partly comes from being the knowledgeable bridge. There are two failure patterns documented at opposite ends here. One is *rubber-stamping* — the Mitra relays the bot's answer to an investor without judgment, and a wrong answer about, say, an incentive eligibility or a clearance requirement goes straight to an investor as if authoritative. The other is *abdication* — over time the Mitra's own grasp of policy atrophies because they lean on the bot. The test worth holding: after a year, can the Udyami Mitra still advise competently if the system is down? You want the bot to make them more capable, not more dependent.

So a question for you: when the bot gives an answer, what is the Udyami Mitra expected to *do* with it — relay it verbatim to the investor, or use it as a reference they then apply judgment to? That decision shapes how you design both the responses and the training.

**On source attribution and authority.** Investment processes are high-stakes — wrong eligibility, wrong incentive calculation, wrong clearance sequence. The pattern that recurs across these deployments is that every claim the system makes should be retrieved from a named departmental source at query time, not answered from the model's memory, and the source should be visible in the response. For your case this does double duty: it protects against confident-wrong answers, and it gives the Udyami Mitra something they can stand behind when an investor pushes back ("this is per the Industrial Investment Policy, clause X").

**On data access — likely your critical path.** You're pulling from across departments. The hard-won lesson from these deployments is that the inter-departmental data-sharing agreements take longer than the technical build, so they should be treated as the critical path and started before building, not in parallel. Who has the authority to authorise that data sharing across departments? Is there a single sponsor senior enough to align them?

Two questions back to you to take this further:

1. How are you thinking about the Udyami Mitra's role with the bot — verbatim relay or judgment layer? 
2. Is there a named senior sponsor (someone at Invest UP / state level) who can authorise cross-departmental data access?
