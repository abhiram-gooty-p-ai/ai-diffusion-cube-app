### Assistant

This is a great question, and there's documented lived experience that maps almost directly onto what you're describing — though from a slightly different starting point worth flagging up front.

The closest parallel is Project Astitva in Nandurbar, Maharashtra, which built voice AI capability for Dehvali Bhili, a tribal language with no prior digital corpus — no written corpus, no recordings, no transcriptions. If Soliga is in a similar position (native speakers, but little or no existing training data), then the core insight from that work is this: **you have to treat language data creation as a prerequisite deployment activity, not a downstream technical task. Build the dataset before you build the application.**

A few things from that experience that I'd surface early, because they're where people get stuck:

**Trust before technology.** Tribal communities are often suspicious of data collection — fears around surveillance, misuse, and loss of control over their own language. Astitva ran repeated field visits, open Q&A sessions, and public events *before* any collection began, and framed participation as contribution (they called it "language donation") rather than extraction. This wasn't a soft add-on; it was the thing that made collection possible at all.

**Who collects, and how it's structured.** They used a community-participatory model — native speakers as contributors, elders as language anchors, youth as validators and digitisers — with a three-tier quality structure (contributors → checkers → validators/subject experts) running production and validation in parallel to maintain quality at speed.

Before I go further into the data and architecture specifics, one question that will shape everything: **does any usable Soliga data exist yet — any recordings, transcriptions, written material — or are you effectively starting from zero?** And roughly how many speakers are you trying to reach, and through what channel (telephone IVR, smartphone app)?

That'll tell me how much of the Astitva playbook applies directly versus what needs adapting.
