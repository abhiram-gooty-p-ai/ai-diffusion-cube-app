### Assistant

That's a good question, but let me push gently on it before answering — because the strongest deployments didn't start from "what can AI solve?" They started from a specific, painful problem and then found AI was the only thing that could address it at scale.

The structural problem that recurs across every agriculture deployment I have evidence for is the same one: the extension system cannot scale with the farmer population. India's baseline was one field officer per 2,000 farmers; Ethiopia's was comparable. That single constraint is why MahaVistaar, Bihar Krishi, Amul, and Ethiopia ATI all exist — farmers can't get timely, personalised advisory not because the knowledge doesn't exist, but because human delivery can't reach everyone. AI was needed for real-time synthesis across fragmented institutional data, in the farmer's language, at the moment of asking.

But the specific problems varied by what each state had: Maharashtra's was fragmentation (university, weather, prices, schemes never talking to each other); Amul's was a data access paradox (50 years of cooperative data the farmer who generated it couldn't see); Bihar's was scheme access for the underserved.

So rather than me hand you a menu, the more useful question for your initiative: do you want each state to identify its own problem, or do you want to offer them a small set of proven problem patterns to choose from? Which fits how your initiative will engage states?
