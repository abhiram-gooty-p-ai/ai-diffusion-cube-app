### Assistant

Let me be straight about what the wiki actually holds versus what you're asking.

**What's documented on tribal/low-resource language pipelines:** Less than you'd want.

The wiki has two relevant reference points:

1. **Bhili is named in MahaVistaar** — MahaVistaar "supports Marathi, Hindi, Bhili, and English." But the pathway page does *not* document *how* Bhili was added: no corpus-building method, no ASR/TTS calibration detail, no data volume, no accuracy benchmarks specific to Bhili. It's listed as a supported language, not documented as a solved problem. That's a real gap for what you're asking.

2. **The general language-pipeline pattern** is documented through Marathi (MahaVistaar) and reused for Oromo (Ethiopia ATI):
   - MahaVistaar built a **bilingual Marathi↔English agricultural glossary** as a core component to handle regional accent and domain terminology (the *Reusable Toolkit* lists this as a transferable asset).
   - The **language pipeline methodology** is documented as transferring "as a starting framework requiring language-specific adaptation" — see [DPG Reuse Patterns](synthesis/dpg-reuse-patterns.md). It is explicitly *not* a turnkey transfer. For Ethiopia, "Oromo and other Ethiopian languages required specific adaptation work."
   - **Dialect variation** is named as one of the two hardest unanticipated challenges in MahaVistaar (alongside API instability) — it took the full nine-month pioneer build to resolve. So a Soliga pipeline should be planned as adaptation-heavy, not plug-in.

**On the infrastructure layer:** the AI Diffusion DPGs are "unbundled and work with any speech and LLM models" (Blue Dots pathway). MahaVistaar integrated **Bhashini, AI4Bharat, and Karya** as language-model partners (the 54-enabler ecosystem map). For a language like Soliga — a Dravidian tribal language with very limited digital corpus — the binding constraint isn't the pipeline architecture, it's whether speech data exists at all.

**What the wiki cannot tell you** — and this is the consequential part:
- How much corpus volume Bhili required, or where it came from
- Whether Bhili was handled by Bhashini/AI4Bharat or built bespoke
- ASR accuracy thresholds for a low-resource language with no standard orthography
- How turn-detection and pronunciation were handled for a language without prior voice-AI work

**My honest recommendation:** This is a case where direct human connection beats the wiki. The people who added Bhili to MahaVistaar — via **EkStep Foundation** (ekstep.org) and the **Bhashini / AI4Bharat / Karya** partners — hold exactly the knowledge you need and the wiki doesn't capture. Karya in particular does low-resource language data collection. Start there with two specific questions:

1. "How was Bhili corpus built and what was the minimum viable data volume?"
2. "Was Soliga-class (sub-100k speaker, no standard script) ever attempted, and what broke?"

One thing worth flagging from the framework before you build: the documented deployments treat **inclusion as a design constraint, not a feature** (shift A5 in [Dimension Shifts](synthesis/dimension-shifts.md)). For Soliga specifically, the harder-to-reach question isn't just language — it's likely connectivity and trust source. Who does the AI speak *as* to a Soliga community? The [Trust Architecture](synthesis/trust-architecture.md) page is clear that voice/language design without genuine institutional attribution produces a system that "feels personal but carries no authority." Worth answering that before the pipeline work.
