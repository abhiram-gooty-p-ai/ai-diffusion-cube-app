### Assistant

Here are the reusable innovations from Blue Dots AI that transfer beyond livelihoods — drawn from the pathway page.

## Architecture-level (B)

**The four-DPG discovery stack** — these are designed as building blocks, not a single-purpose product:

- **Signal DPG** — manages signal creation, location-anchoring, consent-governed storage, and matching/connection flows. Any use case where two sides need to find each other but neither has digital presence (welfare beneficiaries ↔ schemes, patients ↔ services, citizens ↔ government departments) can reuse this. The core innovation: structuring a 2–3 minute voice intent into a location-anchored, matchable signal.
- **Aggregator DPG** — mass onboarding through existing institutional intermediaries, plus status tracking (active, at-risk, satisfied, stalled) and digitally verifiable credential issuance. Reusable wherever you need to onboard a long tail at scale through trusted intermediaries rather than one-by-one.
- **Facilitator DPG** — gives an administration live intelligence on where density is stalling and which use cases are working, replacing static reports. Reusable as a governance/monitoring layer for any ecosystem deployment.

**Build-your-own-data through consent-based voice** — Blue Dots is structurally different from MahaVistaar: neither side existed digitally beforehand. The innovation is building the dataset in real time through 2–3 minute voice calls as participants enrol, rather than waiting for or integrating pre-existing data. Replicable in any context where the data simply does not exist yet.

**Data ownership built into architecture** — participants own their Blue Dot; they can update, pause, or delete. Ownership is architectural, not a policy promise. DPDP-aligned by construction. Reusable as a consent pattern for any citizen-facing system.

## Ecosystem-level (D)

**The aggregator mobilisation pattern** — instead of acquiring participants individually, you onboard them en masse through existing institutional aggregators. In Dharwad, one MSME association onboarded 300+ employers in two weeks; one ITI onboarded 500+ seekers. This cold-start mechanism transfers to any long-tail discovery problem.

**The four-actor ecosystem model** — District Champion + State Sponsor (anchor), Facilitation Team of 6–8 (rhythm), Local Aggregators (mass onboarding), Innovators (services on the rails). This role separation is a replicable template for shared-rails deployments.

## Operating-model-level (F)

**The flywheel / cold-start funding logic** — government funds the cold start (building density before private actors have a reason to join); once innovators find their own economic reasons to engage, the ecosystem self-sustains. In Ghaziabad and Dharwad this threshold hit within three months. This is a reusable economic model for any shared-infrastructure deployment.

**The staleness-management discipline** — the explicit recognition that without active facilitation, signals go cold and both sides lose confidence. The Aggregator DPG surfaces which signals are going cold and which need follow-up. Reusable wherever a two-sided map must stay fresh.

Two caveats worth flagging honestly:

1. **The Blue Dots DPG library is at an earlier codification stage than the OAN agriculture DPGs.** The DPG Reuse Patterns synthesis notes it is "in the same position the OAN agriculture library was in after MahaVistaar" — one pioneer deployment has produced the assets, but cross-context reuse hasn't been proven yet. So these are *candidate* reusable innovations, not yet field-proven across use cases the way the agriculture DPGs are.

2. **The document explicitly frames Blue Dots as horizontal** — applicable to "livelihoods, welfare, agriculture, tourism." So the intent to be cross-use-case is built in, but the evidence is still two districts in one sector.

If you're evaluating these for a specific use case, the most useful next step is a conversation with EkStep Foundation / Tushar Bansal — they hold the district deployment playbook (which sequences who does what across the five lever categories) and can tell you what adaptation your context requires versus what reuses directly.
