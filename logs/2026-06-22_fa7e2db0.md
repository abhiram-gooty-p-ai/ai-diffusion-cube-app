In agriculture and livelihoods deployments in India, voice-first access on any phone — including basic feature phones and landlines — in the user's own language has been the central design choice for reaching exactly this kind of population. A few things they learned that apply to you:

- **Voice-first was treated as the architecture, not an add-on.** The system was built around a phone call from the start, not a smartphone app with voice bolted on. That distinction turned out to matter a lot.
- **Multilingual voice is harder than it looks, in a specific way.** General translation handles everyday speech, but it fails on the *specialised terms* — and in your case, scheme names, eligibility categories, and document names are exactly those specialised terms. One deployment built a domain-specific glossary bridging everyday language and institutional terminology precisely because the general models stumbled here. For you, "minority certificate," "income ceiling," "pre-matric scholarship" and the like are the terms that'll trip up a generic voice system.
