Good — then let's build the full arc. Here's a workshop framework for your 1.5 hours, structured around the decisions that Astitva's experience shows actually determine success. I've suggested rough timings so you can keep the room moving.

**Opening frame (10 min) — Why this, and for whom**
Set the framing that language enablement isn't a technical task — it's opening the door to every service AI can connect to for Soliga speakers. Land two things: who speaks Soliga (how many, where, which dialects), and the *first concrete use case* that justifies the effort (agriculture? health? citizen services?). Astitva started with one language, one deployment, then expanded — so push the room to pick a single anchor use case, not a wishlist.

**Segment 1 (20 min) — The actor map and the orchestrator**
This is where you assemble who needs to be in the room. Use Astitva's actor set as a checklist: community speakers, elders as language anchors, youth as validators, a data collection platform, linguistic validators, a model builder, hosting infrastructure, a convening authority, and domain advisors. Two decisions to force here: *who is the convening authority* (the equivalent of the District Collector — someone with the standing to mobilise community and departments), and *who plays the neutral orchestrator* that connects the technical model-builders to the field effort. The Astitva failure to flag: IIT Madras didn't know the effort was happening — connection didn't happen by itself.

**Segment 2 (25 min) — The data problem (the hardest part)**
This deserves the most time because it's where the effort lives or dies. Walk through:
- *Trust before collection* — communities are often wary of data collection (surveillance, misuse fears). Astitva ran field visits, open Q&A, and framed participation as donation (Tribaldaan), not extraction. Compensation fair and tied to verified contribution.
- *Quality structure* — a three-tier model (contributors → checkers → validators/experts) with production and validation running in parallel, not sequentially.
- *Two non-negotiable technical lessons* — collect conversational/spontaneous speech separately from read-aloud (models trained only on read speech fail in real conversation); and collect at telephony quality (8kHz), not just clean 16kHz audio, or the model degrades on live calls. Also: make sure all dialects are represented in both collection and testing.

**Segment 3 (15 min) — Architecture and first deployment**
Lighter touch, but decide: component architecture (ASR + LLM + TTS) rather than end-to-end speech-to-speech, because you'll need domain control and guardrails. Avoid single-vendor lock-in — Astitva and the broader voice AI experience both stress running multiple vendors in parallel during pilot. And confirm whether your use case needs outbound calling (system calls citizen) as well as inbound — because that shapes the architecture and is expensive to retrofit later.

**Segment 4 (15 min) — Long-term home and sustainability**
This is the ownership question you wanted to include. Astitva's answer was a deliberate multi-tier handoff: **district builds, state sustains, national makes accessible.** Concretely — the language capability got integrated into the state platform (its operational home), and the dataset was published to Bhashini as open national infrastructure, so the asset doesn't depend on any single vendor staying engaged and the community retains ownership. For your session, force the question: *once the Soliga capability exists, who runs it, who updates it when it degrades, and where does the dataset live so it survives the people who built it?*

**Close (5 min) — Name the first three moves**
End by having the room commit to: the anchor use case, the convening authority/orchestrator, and the first data collection step.

One honest caveat worth carrying into the room: all of this lived experience comes from Dehvali Bhili in Maharashtra. Soliga's dialect spread, the community's relationship with government, and Karnataka's institutional setup will differ — so treat Astitva as a tested template to adapt, not a recipe to copy.
