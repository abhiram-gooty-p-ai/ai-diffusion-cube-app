This is a great use case to ground in lived experience — there's a documented deployment that did almost exactly this. Project Astitva in Nandurbar, Maharashtra, enabled Dehvali Bhili (a low-resource tribal language with no prior digital dataset) for voice AI and integrated it into the state agricultural advisory system. The whole initial phase ran in about a month, so the structure they used maps well onto a workshop where you're trying to arrive at an approach.

Let me suggest a framework for your 1.5 hours, drawn from how Astitva actually approached it. I'd anchor the brainstorm around a few decisions that, in their experience, determined whether the effort succeeded or stalled:

**1. Who are you actually serving, and to unlock what?** Astitva's framing insight is worth starting with — enabling a language isn't a technical task, it's opening the door to *every* service that AI can connect to for Soliga speakers. So before voice tech, get clear on: who speaks Soliga, where, and what's the first concrete use case (agricultural advisory? health? citizen services?) that justifies the effort. They started with one language, one deployment (agriculture via the existing extension platform), then planned expansion.

**2. The data problem — because it's the hardest part.** Soliga, like Dehvali Bhili, almost certainly has no usable corpus. This is "zero to one." Two field-tested lessons that should shape your discussion here: collect *conversational* speech separately from read-aloud speech (models trained only on read speech fail in real conversation), and collect at *telephony audio quality* (8kHz), not just clean smartphone audio (16kHz) — otherwise the model degrades on live calls. These two mistakes were discovered the hard way and are worth surfacing early.

I'll pause there before laying out the rest — does your workshop need to cover the full path through to deployment and sustainability, or are you mainly trying to crack the language-enablement approach itself? That'll shape how I'd weight the remaining time.
