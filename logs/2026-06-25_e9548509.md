### Assistant

This is a great use case to ground in lived experience — there's a documented deployment that did almost exactly this. Project Astitva in Nandurbar, Maharashtra, enabled Dehvali Bhili (a low-resource tribal language with no prior digital dataset) for voice AI and integrated it into the state agricultural advisory system. The whole initial phase ran in about a month, so the structure they used maps well onto a workshop where you're trying to arrive at an approach.

Let me suggest a framework for your 1.5 hours, drawn from how Astitva actually approached it. I'd anchor the brainstorm around a few decisions that, in their experience, determined whether the effort succeeded or stalled:

**1. Who are you actually serving, and to unlock what?** Astitva's framing insight is worth starting with — enabling a language isn't a technical task, it's opening the door to *every* service that AI can connect to for Soliga speakers. So before voice tech, get clear on: who speaks Soliga, where, and what's the first concrete use case (agricultural advisory? health? citizen services?) that justifies the effort. They started with one language, one deployment (agriculture via the existing extension platform), then planned expansion.

**2. The data problem — because it's the hardest part.** Soliga, like Dehvali Bhili, almost certainly has no usable corpus. This is "zero to one." Two field-tested lessons that should shape your discussion here: collect *conversational* speech separately from read-aloud speech (models trained only on read speech fail in real conversation), and collect at *telephony audio quality* (8kHz), not just clean smartphone audio (16kHz) — otherwise the model degrades on live calls. These two mistakes were discovered the hard way and are worth surfacing early.

I'll pause there before laying out the rest — does your workshop need to cover the full path through to deployment and sustainability, or are you mainly trying to crack the language-enablement approach itself? That'll shape how I'd weight the remaining time.

### Assistant

You're exactly right — and that's a sharp catch. The institution and partner questions are not unique to Option B. They're load-bearing even in Option A.

Astitva makes this clear. Just to *create* the Dehvali Bhili dataset and build the language capability, they needed a whole set of actors in the room: community speakers, a data collection platform provider (Karya), linguistic validators, a model builder (IIT Madras), hosting infrastructure (Bhashini), and crucially — a convening authority (the District Collector) who could mobilise multiple line departments and the community simultaneously. None of that is "deployment." It's all upstream of having a working voice model at all.

In fact, one of the most important lessons from that pathway is about a coordination gap *within* the enablement phase: IIT Madras didn't even know the Nandurbar effort was happening — there was no mechanism connecting the technical model-builders to the field effort. The takeaway was that a **neutral orchestrator** — someone whose job is to map all the required actors and connect them — is essential. Without it, the people who need to be collaborating are solving the same problem in isolation, or aren't in the room at all.

So you're correct: institution and partners matter in both. Let me draw the real distinction more carefully:

- **In Option A**, the institution/partner question is: *who needs to be assembled to produce the dataset and model* — and who convenes them.
- **In Option B**, you add a second layer: *who owns the language capability long-term once it exists*, and who sustains it when the initial push ends. Astitva's answer was a deliberate handoff — district builds, state sustains (via the agricultural platform), national makes it accessible (dataset published to Bhashini as open infrastructure). That handoff is the "ownership" piece I clumsily referred to.

So the honest framing for your workshop is probably: **enabling Soliga for voice AI requires the institutional and ecosystem thinking from the start — the only question is whether you also want to resolve the long-term home and sustainability of the capability in the same session.**
