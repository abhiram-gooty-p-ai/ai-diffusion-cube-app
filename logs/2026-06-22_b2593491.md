### Assistant

That's a much sharper problem — and it has a familiar shape. One person responsible for far more people than they can individually attend to is precisely the structural constraint behind every agriculture deployment I have evidence on. In Bihar, the baseline was one agriculture field officer for every 2,000 farmers. The insight there was important: AI didn't replace the field officer, and it didn't try to be the expert. It changed what the scarce human spent their time on — handling the routine queries at scale so the officer could focus on the cases that actually needed judgment.

That distinction matters a lot for your situation, because there are two quite different things "AI for one-teacher-sixty-kids" could mean, and they lead to very different places:

One is AI facing the *child* directly — a tutor each kid interacts with. The other is AI supporting the *teacher* — helping her see which of her sixty children are stuck and where, so she can direct her limited attention to the right four kids today rather than spreading it evenly and thinly across all sixty. The agriculture deployments lean heavily toward the second model, and there's a hard-won reason for it: the recurring warning across those deployments is that AI should make the frontline person's skill *more precise*, not let it atrophy. A teacher who knows how to teach but can't see where each child is — AI can fix the seeing without replacing the teaching.

So a question worth sitting with: is the bottleneck that children need more instruction, or that the teacher can't *diagnose and prioritise* across sixty kids fast enough to teach the right thing to the right child? Those point at different builds.

I'd be honest that I can't show you an education deployment that did either — that's genuinely a gap in what I know. But if the teacher-support framing resonates, the patterns around how you'd train her to use it, and how you'd make sure she stays in the loop rather than rubber-stamping whatever the system says, do transfer. Which of those two directions feels closer to what's actually broken in your classrooms?
