Let me unpack it, because it's really three separate design signals, all answering one question: when the user first encounters the system, does it feel built *for them*, or does it feel like something they have to adapt themselves to reach?

A farmer asking about a sick crop in her own dialect, and being understood, is a trust signal. Being asked to rephrase in standard language is the opposite.

**Channel** — can the user reach it on what they actually have? Amul's producers are largely rural women on basic feature phones. So the system is reachable by a plain voice call, including on a landline — no smartphone, no app, no data plan. The contrast: a system that requires a smartphone and an internet connection silently excludes the very people who most need it. The channel choice signals who you built for before a single word is exchanged.

**Persona** — does the system present itself as one of them, or as an institution they're petitioning? Amul named theirs "Sarlaben" — a Gujarati woman's name. That choice signals "this is built for the woman producer, in her terms," before she's asked her first question. Calling a number that feels like calling a person carries a different trust weight than navigating a government portal.

The deeper point underneath all three: there's a difference between a system the user is *calling* and a system built *for* the user calling. These three signals are what tip it from the first to the second — and they're decided before the AI gives a single answer.
