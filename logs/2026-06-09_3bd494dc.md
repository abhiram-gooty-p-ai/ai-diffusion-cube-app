**MahaVistaar — Maharashtra's state agriculture voice AI**

The pioneer deployment on the OpenAgriNet pathway. Built in nine months by the Department of Agriculture, Maharashtra, with EkStep Foundation and OpenAgriNet.

**What it does:** Connects crop advisories, weather, market prices, government schemes, and grievance tracking through a single voice call (short code 155313) on any phone, in Marathi, Hindi, Bhili, and English. Both inbound (farmers call with questions) and outbound (proactive pest alerts, crop calendar advisories, weather warnings).

**The problem it solved:** Fragmentation. The state agricultural university held crop knowledge, IMD held weather, APMCs held prices, MahaDBT held scheme status — none talked to each other, and none was reachable by a smallholder farmer through a single call. A historical baseline of roughly one field officer per 2,000 farmers made human-only advisory structurally impossible at scale.

**Numbers:** 342,000+ unique users; 1.67 million+ questions answered; 791,000+ sessions; 17 lakh farmers reached daily through proactive voice alerts; 97–98.5% positive feedback.

**Architecture highlights:**
- Seven-layer OAN architecture; connects to data where it lives rather than centralising (sovereignty + DPDP compliance)
- Self-hosted fine-tuned Qwen3.5-27B on 4×H100 GPUs (vLLM) as primary, Azure OpenAI as fallback — ~180× cost reduction (₹9.4/question → ₹0.05/question at full utilisation)
- Independent moderation model (GPT-OSS Safeguard 20B), fully decoupled from the advisory engine
- Peak load policy: "latency over queue depth" — cap concurrency, route overflow to Azure rather than queue

**Why it matters most:** It's the asset generator. Every transferable asset subsequent deployments reused — architecture, governance frameworks, language pipeline methodology, data connector approach, model evaluation benchmarks, failure mode library — was built here. That's why Ethiopia compressed to three months and Amul to three weeks.

**Gaps in the page worth noting:** Workforce dimension (E) is thin — extension officer training design, resistance, and the agency/dependency question are largely "not documented." Same for how it got approved and funded, and the pilot-to-steady-state transition structure.

Honest answer first: the wiki documents **that** MahaVistaar decoupled them and treats it as a deliberate, maintained design choice — but it does not spell out a full rationale for *why* decoupling matters. So let me give you what the wiki actually grounds, and flag where I'm reaching beyond it.

**What the wiki documents (MahaVistaar Architecture Note, B dimension):**

- The moderation model (GPT-OSS Safeguard 20B, 2×H100s) and the advisory model (fine-tuned Qwen3.5-27B, 4×H100s) are "fully decoupled — a design choice the team explicitly maintained."
- The moderation layer performs domain validation, content safety filtering, prompt injection defence, and input sanitisation on every query.
- In voice channels, moderation is embedded within the ASR pipeline.
- The design principle stated: unsafe outputs are caught *before* they reach the farmer, not addressed by policy after the fact.

**What the architecture implies (grounded in the wiki, but my inference):**

1. **Independence of judgment.** If the same model both generates advice and judges its own safety, it shares the same blind spots and failure modes. A separate model — different weights, different training — gives an independent check. This is consistent with the "moderation-before-output" principle the Failure Modes page names as the preventive architecture against the *harmful output reaches user* failure mode.

2. **Swappability.** B1 in the Dimension Shifts page — "model as a replaceable component." Decoupling means you can upgrade or swap the advisory LLM (Qwen → something better) without re-validating your safety layer, and vice versa. They evolve on independent clocks.

3. **Different operational placement.** The wiki notes moderation runs inside the ASR pipeline for voice and runs on its own GPU allocation. They're not just logically separate — they're physically and pipeline-separate, which is what lets them scale and fail independently.

**Where the wiki goes quiet:** It does not document specific incidents where decoupling caught something a coupled system would have missed, nor the latency cost of the extra model call, nor how the team calibrated the moderation model's thresholds. The adversarial test set (up to 500 attack patterns) is mentioned as the calibration tool, but the calibration process itself isn't detailed.

If you want the actual engineering reasoning rather than the architecture rationale, the **MahaVistaar Architecture Note (May 2026)** is the source, and **EkStep Foundation** is the contact — this is a case where talking to the people who made the decoupling decision will give you more than the wiki holds.
