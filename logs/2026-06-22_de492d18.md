Here's what the experience says on the language side:

**Build the multilingual speech pipeline as core infrastructure, not a post-launch feature.** MahaVistaar treated speech recognition and text-to-speech for regional languages as a foundational layer from day one, not something added once the system worked in one language. The reason is structural — retrofitting language support into a system designed around one language tends to mean rebuilding. So whichever languages your citizens speak, they're a design input at the start, not a roadmap item.

**The dialect problem is real and was underestimated.** MahaVistaar's two hardest operational surprises over its nine-month build were dialect variation and API instability. Citizens don't speak the textbook version of a language — they speak it with regional accent, local vocabulary, and code-switching (sliding between, say, Hindi and English mid-sentence). General-purpose speech models degrade badly on this. For your grievance use case this bites twice: once on understanding the citizen, and again on understanding the domain — a citizen describing a drainage problem or a pension delay uses words that don't map cleanly to your departmental categories.

**The fix that recurs: a domain-specific bilingual glossary.** This is the bridge between how citizens actually describe their problems and the institutional terminology your departments use. It's built per language pair, per domain. General translation models fail on specialised terms — so this glossary is the thing that lets "my widow pension hasn't come" land correctly against the right scheme and the right department. MahaVistaar built a Marathi↔English agricultural glossary as a core component precisely because of this.

**One genuinely transferable lesson from a low-resource language effort** (Project Astitva, which built voice AI for a tribal language with no prior digital corpus): if any of your citizen languages are low-resource — languages where good speech models simply don't exist yet — that's not a technical task you slot in late. It becomes a prerequisite activity: you have to create the language data before you can build the application on it. Worth auditing your language list early against what models already exist, so you know which languages are "available" versus which ones need foundational work.
