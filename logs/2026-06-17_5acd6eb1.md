Yes. The wiki holds direct evidence for exactly your situation — a state agriculture department deploying AI. The clearest reference is **MahaVistaar** (Maharashtra), the pioneer deployment, and **Bihar Krishi**, a state department that built independently then connected to the national layer.

Let me give you the starting points the evidence supports.

**First, the two decisions that unlock everything else** (from the dimension-shifts synthesis):

1. **Who is the network operator?** One institution must be able to say "this runs in my name" and align all the partners. In MahaVistaar this was the Department of Agriculture authorising data-sharing across its own systems and universities. This is your role to anchor. Without it, deployments stall in coordination before they stall in technology.

2. **Framing — project or transformation?** Bihar embedded its deployment in its 4th Agriculture Roadmap, not as a standalone tech project. This is what secured multi-year funding and survived staff changes. A deployment framed as a tech project gets evaluated as one — time-bounded, no workforce training, no life after the pilot.

**What you do not need to build from scratch:**
The OpenAgriNet (OAN) DPG library already contains the architecture, governance frameworks, language pipeline methodology, data connector approach, and failure mode library. MahaVistaar took 9 months as a pioneer; Ethiopia reused these assets in 3 months; Amul in 3 weeks. Your first step should be a **DPG audit** with EkStep Foundation (ekstep.org) — the question is not "what do I build?" but "what applies to my state, and what specifically needs adapting?"

**The structural realities for a state department:**
- **Data**: It already exists, fragmented across your department, agricultural universities, IMD, APMCs, scheme systems. The task is *connection*, not collection. The OAN principle is data stays where it lives; AI connects at query time. This also keeps you DPDP-compliant by design.
- **Access**: Design voice-first in your state language for feature phones — not as a later add-on. This is what made MahaVistaar reach farmers at extension-impossible ratios.
- **Workforce**: Bihar trained 15,000+ extension workers across 38 districts as part of the rollout, not after it. Budget for this as a deployment dependency.
- **Trust**: The AI must speak as your department, with your agricultural university providing advisory authority. Farmers trust the institution, not the AI.

**The 70/30 warning**: ~70% of the challenge is non-technology (institution, ecosystem, workforce, operating model). If your plan has three pages on model selection and one paragraph on governance, it's misaligned with where deployments actually fail.
