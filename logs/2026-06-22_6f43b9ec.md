Let me lay out what the pathway documents on architecture — and then flag where the line between shared infrastructure and end-user apps gets blurry in the documentation.

**The shared infrastructure — four DPGs**

Blue Dots runs on four open-source building blocks:

2. **Signal DPG** — manages each Blue Dot signal. It structures a signal as a location-anchored Blue Dot, stores it under consent-governed rules, handles matching and connection flows, and feeds live signals out to consuming platforms.

3. **Aggregator DPG** — gives each aggregator (an ITI, MSME association, NGO) a platform to register, onboard participants at scale, track Blue Dot status (active, at-risk, satisfied, stalled), and issue digitally verifiable credentials.

4. **Facilitator DPG** — gives the district administration tools to monitor the supply-demand gap, onboard aggregators and innovators, and keep the ecosystem healthy.

So the shared rails are: **voice intake → signal management/matching/storage → aggregator onboarding tooling → district monitoring tooling.** Data lives on these shared public rails, operated by the state department, governed by the district, owned by participants.

**What's an end-user app vs the shared layer**

Here's where it's clearer in principle than in documented detail. The architecture is explicitly designed so that **innovators plug into the shared map** — staffing firms, assessment providers, skilling organisations, transport and financial services providers, startups. Named ones in the pilots: Head Held High, Recex, JobsUp, TRRAIN, Proof of Skill, Digital Labour Chowk. These are the end-user-facing applications. They consume the live signals from the Signal DPG and build their own services on top.

So the division is roughly:
- **Shared layer (the rails):** voice intake, Blue Dot creation, storage, matching, status tracking, district monitoring — the four DPGs
- **App layer (built by innovators):** the actual services participants and businesses interact with for placement, assessment, skilling, transport, finance

And the voice call itself is interesting — it's both. A participant *becomes* a Blue Dot through the shared voice layer directly (that's not an innovator app), but then innovators build richer services on the resulting signals.
