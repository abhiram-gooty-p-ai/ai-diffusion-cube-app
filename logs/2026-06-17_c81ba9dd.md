What transfers — and this is the strong part — is the technology and the codified know-how. Architecture, governance frameworks, language pipeline methodology, model evaluation benchmarks, deployment playbooks, failure-mode libraries. This is exactly what made the nine-months-to-three-weeks compression happen. Your initiative can own and maintain this shared pool.

What does *not* transfer, and must be rebuilt in each state: the ecosystem of partners, the institutional owner who says "this runs in my name," the language/dialect calibration, and the specific data-access arrangements. Maharashtra needed 54 partners; Ethiopia needed an entirely different set. The four-layer ecosystem *structure* is reusable; the actual partners are not.

So a clean-slate design is realistic for the shared infrastructure layer. But there's one thing the documented evidence is emphatic about that you can't design away from the centre: every successful deployment had a single institution at the state level accountable for it. That's the part most multi-state initiatives underestimate.

Before we go further into design — who is the deploying entity in each state meant to be? Have you thought about that yet?
